Liang Chen

Retired lift operator & amateur portrait sitter

Liang Chen, seventy-nine, spent decades operating a hillside goods lift. Now he sits for a drawing class, curious to see which version of his face will appear on the page.

Background

East Asian; Chinese-family background within Glasssteppe’s established railway community.

A life in detail

328 words

For years Liang measured mornings by loads arriving at the lift: crates waiting in the wrong place, a delayed consignment, someone certain there was room for one more item. He learned to begin on time and dislikes being kept waiting. Retirement has preserved that punctuality while supplying stranger appointments. For his first portrait sitting, he arrived early enough to watch the students argue amiably over the lamps.

One drawing gave him a smoother forehead than the face he saw while shaving. Liang asked the artist to put the lines back. He enjoys the variety between portraits: a severe mouth in one, an unexpectedly mischievous gaze in another. Holding still is harder than he expected. During a break he sometimes leans toward a half-finished page and offers an opinion nobody requested.

Liang is the father of Mei Chen and her younger sister Qiao. Mei’s adult child Ren and Qiao’s seventeen-year-old son Jian Wu bring different news to family visits. Chinese-language correspondence and railway addresses filled drawers in his childhood home; he still recognizes relatives by the way they wrote a street name. He likes hearing Mei’s choir, although his suggestions from the back row have earned him a pointed look.

His advice recently caused a sharper exchange with Qiao. Hearing about the laundry’s costs, he proposed pursuing bigger contracts without listening to what longer hours were costing her. She told him that concern was not a business plan. He first defended his experience, then found himself rehearsing an apology on the walk home. He has yet to deliver it without attaching another suggestion.

Glasssteppe’s close-set streets suit him, provided he leaves time for the climbs and chooses a sheltered place to pause. He wants a portrait of the person inhabiting those streets now, including his hollowing temples and stubborn expression. The next sitting is already in his diary. So is a visit to Qiao, where he hopes to ask about her weekend before discussing anything that could be improved.

Liang likes an appointment kept and an opinion made useful. His directing instinct can outrun his invitation to contribute; sitting for artists gives that decisive habit an unfamiliar, receptive role.

Everyday responses

What matters most
To remain a participant with a present life, have his word count and be seen accurately rather than ceremonially. He wants closeness without admitting how often he tries to direct it.
Communication
A definite observation followed too quickly by a recommendation. Dry comments and specific memories warm his voice; a rare question without a suggested answer signals real attention.
Decisions
Choose a sensible course, make the appointment and expect the agreement to hold. He is more flexible about how an artist sees him than about how a relative runs a task.
Building trust
Keep an agreed time, tell him something specific you noticed and disagree without turning him into a family caricature. Approval is less important than taking him seriously.
Conflict & repair
He initially defends intention. Repair begins when he names the unrequested advice, asks Qiao what she wanted and tolerates an answer he would not have chosen.
Under stress
Repeats advice in slightly different words, as though misunderstanding must explain refusal. He may declare a conversation settled before its other participant has agreed.
Giving & receiving care
Shows up and attends closely. His more useful care sometimes means staying in the room while another person makes the decision, instead of supplying the plan.
A useful contradiction
He likes hearing several artists disagree about his face and dislikes hearing his daughter disagree about her own business.

A continuing home

Glasssteppe is his longstanding home, with familiar routes, family and a drawing class that lets his life keep acquiring new experiences.

Where it can feel less comfortable

The climbing city asks him to budget energy. Familiarity also tempts neighbors and relatives to put him in the permanent role of adviser or keeper of tradition.

In a small moment

A drawing makes his face look older than he expected.

That is my face today. Show me what caught your eye.

He looks again instead of asking the artist to flatter him.

Qiao says she did not ask for advice.

No, you didn’t. I decided the question for you. What were you telling me?

He stops proposing fixes and lets her restart.
