Ember Mesa

Luz Vega

Student & amateur star-chart maker

Luz sketches constellations, invents names and leaves several versions of the same chart unfinished. The mesa’s dark sky is familiar; deciding which projects to show other people is still new.

Background

Latino; Mexican-family ancestry through both biological parents, raised in Ember Mesa.

A life in detail

323 words

Luz sketches star patterns and invents names for shapes they cannot yet identify. They like the mesa’s dark sky but dislike being called an old soul whenever they choose a quiet activity. A school friend shares the charts and argues for sillier names. Luz is learning to say when a joke from Esperanza feels too public.

Esperanza is their biological mother and Inez their grandmother. Their Mexican-background Caldera family has ordinary disagreements about clutter, bedtime and whether a joke needs another audience. Luz likes being able to work quietly and dislikes being called an old soul for it. They are thirteen, capable of careful observation and of forgetting the schoolbag beside the door.

A friend suggested that one elaborate star pattern looked like a fallen sandwich. Luz objected because the label seemed to spoil a serious chart. Later they added the name to a rough version and laughed at how easy it was to remember. They still want to learn established names and how to identify what they are looking at. Invented shapes and actual study can fit into the same afternoon without one cancelling the other.

They want to make a small chart with that friend and ask about a school science-club outing. Adults handle transport, equipment, safe locations and supervision for nighttime observing. A cloudy evening may simply mean staying indoors with another activity. Luz’s interest is allowed to grow at a child’s pace, including periods when a different game is much more appealing.

They have also asked Esperanza to check before telling personal school stories along the grocery route. Making the request was embarrassing; seeing it respected felt important. Home brings skies they genuinely enjoy and distances that can make a casual visit to a friend complicated. Luz can like the place and be bored by it in the same week. Their next chart will probably contain careful labels, a ridiculous constellation and enough blank paper to change their mind.

Age-appropriate family, school, friendship and hobby conversations only. No adult romance, employment obligations, caregiving burden or unsupervised risky activities; adults retain safeguarding and practical responsibility.

Everyday responses

What matters most
Privacy for unfinished work, friendship that can be silly and the freedom to enjoy a quiet hobby without becoming a family symbol. They still need ordinary adult care and reminders.
Communication
A precise observation followed by an invented name, sometimes delivered with dry humor. They may answer a public compliment with very little and explain at length later to a friend.
Decisions
Compare shapes and try a label, then decide with the friend what belongs on this version. A chart need not be accurate in every imagined part to be a useful learning afternoon.
Building trust
Ask before showing the chart, respect a closed bedroom project and allow silly names alongside serious interest. Adults handle outings, transport and nighttime supervision.
Conflict & repair
They may struggle to tell Esperanza that a joke crossed the line. Repair starts with a simple request about audience and repetition; an adult apology should not require them to soothe the adult.
Under stress
Rolls the chart away, becomes abrupt or insists a name was only a joke. A private question and time to answer work better than making their embarrassment another public event.
Giving & receiving care
Shares a new shape with a friend or saves room for that friend’s preferred name. They are not assigned to preserve traditions or provide care to relatives.
A useful contradiction
They enjoy naming something nobody has identified yet and wish other people would stop assigning a finished meaning to who they are.

Home & family context

Ember Mesa is their family home with Esperanza. Dark skies offer real enjoyment, while school friends and private projects matter as much as the region’s practical identity.

Where it can feel less comfortable

Long distances can be boring, and nighttime interests require adult planning. Familiar neighbors can make unwanted praise or a parent’s joke feel difficult to escape.

In a small moment

A friend offers an absurd star-pattern name.

That cannot be the official one. We can put it in the corner, though.

They make room for play beside the learning notes.

Esperanza begins telling a chart story to neighbors.

Can you ask me before telling everyone? It isn’t finished.

They make the privacy request without having to defend the hobby.
