Pelagia

Yuna Park

Community radio host

Yuna Park hosts a neighborhood radio hour in Pelagia. A recent arrival from Glasssteppe, she is learning the harbor through the voices and ordinary sounds people choose to share.

Background

Korean-family background; raised in Glasssteppe and now a newcomer to Pelagia.

A life in detail

316 words

Yuna Park is thirty-seven and has recently made a home in Pelagia after growing up in Glasssteppe. Her Korean-background family belongs to the highland railway community, where relatives still live. She brings familiar words, family recipes, and an instinct to describe distance by how many stairs a journey contains. Pelagia’s flat walkways and changing tide offer a different kind of geography to learn, even before she has learned where everything is.

Her radio work began with short recorded introductions for a neighborhood listening evening. Hearing her own hurried voice played back made her realize how much she had tried to fit into a single breath. She started writing shorter notes and allowing a pause before the next subject. The practice grew into community radio, where a useful introduction can make room for another person’s voice without explaining their whole life in advance.

In Pelagia she hosts a neighborhood hour that includes conversation, music selected with permission, and small recordings people have agreed to share. A market opening and an evening ferry sound different through a microphone than they do while someone is hurrying past. Yuna is interested in those differences, but she asks before recording a person and checks how a contribution should be credited. Her listeners include longtime harbor residents and people who, like her, are still learning the local names.

Moving has given her routines that sit beside the radio work. She sends family messages to Glasssteppe, experiments with where her old books will fit, and sometimes chooses a familiar supper after a day full of introductions. She can be animated during a broadcast and need a quiet walk afterward. Being new does not make every encounter a grand discovery; some days she simply wants to remember the correct turn. Her place in Pelagia is taking shape through repeated journeys and conversations, with her highland family history remaining part of an ordinary, changing life.

Everyday responses

What matters most
Connection, room to make good work and a feeling of belonging that does not depend on being an endlessly interesting newcomer. She needs private familiarity as well as public contact.
Communication
Vivid opening detail, an inviting question and a tendency to connect subjects quickly. Her practiced voice leaves a beat for an answer; her excited voice sometimes needs to be reminded.
Decisions
Ask what makes the contribution understandable and who gets to define it. She checks permission and credit before deciding whether a pleasing recording belongs in the program.
Building trust
Correct a local name kindly, let her be ordinary and distinguish friendly conversation from material for a broadcast. She opens up when she need not host the interaction.
Conflict & repair
She may smooth over a disagreement too soon. Real repair means allowing the objection to stay audible, correcting a credit or wording and asking before recording anything further.
Under stress
She overexplains, fills silence or tries to repair an awkward moment with another cheerful introduction. More stimulation can make her feel less connected rather than more.
Giving & receiving care
Make a clear introduction when welcome, remember what a person wants called by its own name and offer company with no microphone. She must resist turning comfort into an interview.
A useful contradiction
She is skilled at making other people feel introduced and can still feel conspicuously new herself.

A chosen home

Her recent Pelagian move gives her radio work conversations between longtime residents and people learning the harbor. The compact waterfront offers repeat encounters that can become familiarity.

Where it can feel less comfortable

Flat walkways and tides require a different map from highland stairs. A day of introductions can leave her wanting a familiar supper and a quiet walk rather than another discovery.

In a small moment

A contributor corrects her introduction.

Thank you. How would you like me to say it?

She replaces her polished sentence instead of defending it.

A friend proposes another lively evening after a broadcast.

I think I've used all my introductions. Could we just walk?

She makes a quieter request without treating the friendship as work.
