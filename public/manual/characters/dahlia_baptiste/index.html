Alder Quay

Dahlia Baptiste

Parcel-depot supervisor & amateur sprinter

Dahlia can untangle a missed-delivery queue and organize a crowded household. The running track is where she has started asking what she wants for herself.

Background

Black Afro-Atlantic Bellweather family, shared with Celia, Eli, Imani and Noel.

A life in detail

309 words

Dahlia handles missed deliveries and shift swaps at the parcel depot. She had Imani at twenty-two and Noel nine years later. After years of organizing everybody’s evenings she joined a track club; her second marriage to Malik includes explicit agreements about shared domestic work.

She is Celia’s younger child and Eli’s sister, part of the same Black Afro-Atlantic Bellweather family. Her children, Imani and Noel, share another biological father outside the household. Malik is their stepfather. Dahlia and Malik have been married four years; the division of shopping, pickups and evening meals is something they discuss explicitly, even when a discussion arrives at an inconvenient hour.

At the depot, she once volunteered to cover a late shift before remembering that she had promised to watch Noel’s first school film showing. She found another supervisor, accepted the awkwardness of asking, and arrived just before the lights went down. The incident embarrassed her because nobody had forced the original offer. Being the fastest person to solve a problem had become a habit she could mistake for generosity.

Her track club gives her a different measure of progress. A disappointing time can annoy her for an evening without threatening anyone’s supper or wages. She likes the clean pleasure of a good start and the gossip during the cooldown. Training twice a week is a commitment she protects, although rain, a crowded shift and ordinary fatigue sometimes defeat it.

Dahlia wants the depot promotion, including the pay and authority, while refusing a position that quietly means permanent availability. Alder is where her friends, family and working knowledge are concentrated, and staying has been a deliberate choice. Familiar neighbors can make ambition feel oddly public. She is learning to state it plainly. When she snaps at somebody who did not deserve it, her best repair is usually a direct apology and a changed plan.

Everyday responses

What matters most
She needs ambition to be taken seriously, shared domestic effort and time with a purpose of her own. Promotion interests her for pay and authority as well as recognition.
Communication
She leads with the problem, the needed action and a time. Explanations become shorter when tired. Among runners she can be chatty; not every conversation has to earn its place on a schedule.
Decisions
She favors a workable answer now over a perfect one later. The danger is volunteering before asking whether she actually wants the task or has already promised that hour elsewhere.
Building trust
Follow through on a shared job without requiring supervision. She respects people who state their limits early and who can tell her a fast solution has overlooked somebody.
Conflict & repair
She may begin by defending the pressure she was under. Her stronger repair is a short, unqualified apology followed by a changed arrangement; gifts would let her avoid the actual problem.
Under stress
She accelerates, interrupts and treats additional questions as obstacles. A helpful suggestion can sound to her like one more decision to manage. Running cannot reliably erase an overloaded day.
Giving & receiving care
She removes practical obstacles and shows up for important moments. Affection becomes more legible when she stays after the immediate problem is solved rather than rushing toward the next rescue.
A useful contradiction
She wants a life that includes freely chosen work, yet proving that she can handle everything remains an easy way to surrender her choices.

A continuing home

Alder concentrates friends, family and the working knowledge she has built. Compact streets support busy ordinary days; choosing to stay is compatible with wanting a larger role.

Where it can feel less comfortable

Familiarity makes a promotion or family strain feel publicly discussable. She sometimes downplays what she wants, then resents others for not recognizing it.

In a small moment

A depot discussion circles without a decision.

What are we deciding today? Give me that bit first.

She lists the two live options, then makes herself hear the objection to her preferred one.

She snaps during a household interruption.

That was sharp. You asked a normal question. Start again.

She stops sorting the parcels or bags in front of her and gives the answer her attention.
