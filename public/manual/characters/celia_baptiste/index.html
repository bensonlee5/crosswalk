Alder Quay

Celia Baptiste

Retired housing clerk & competitive domino player

Celia spent thirty years keeping housing records straight. Retirement has made room for fierce domino games, experimental paintings and a firmer answer when somebody assumes she is free.

Background

Black Afro-Atlantic Bellweather family; biological mother of Eli and Dahlia.

A life in detail

308 words

Celia kept tenancy records for thirty years and now plays dominoes at the covered ferry hall. She raised Eli and Dahlia in a small quay flat. She loves a decisive win, dislikes being treated as everybody’s available grandmother, and occasionally repeats a confidence when a story gets too good.

The Baptistes belong to Alder’s long-established Black Afro-Atlantic community. Celia is grandmother to Asha through Eli, and to Imani and Noel through Dahlia. She remembers their preferences with considerable accuracy, then resents the suggestion that remembering them means she should organize every gathering. Two agreed school pickups a month suit her. An expanding list of favors does not.

At the ferry hall, she recently lost a doubles match because she refused to abandon an ambitious opening. She blamed the noise, then the table, before admitting that her partner had warned her. They stayed for another game. Her competitiveness can make an afternoon sharper than necessary, but she brings the same wholehearted delight to an opponent’s genuinely clever move.

A spare room in her flat holds family boxes and the beginnings of several paintings. Celia has set a collection date for the boxes, written it twice, and left space by the window for a proper easel. She does not yet know whether she enjoys painting or simply enjoys wanting something that nobody else needs. She is willing to find out badly, with cheap brushes and uneven results.

Alder’s dense streets and covered landings let her see familiar people without arranging a whole excursion. She loves that ease and sometimes finds it oppressive. When a neighbor repeated a confidence she had carelessly passed along, she had to make an uncomfortable apology. Celia can be funny, affectionate and indiscreet in the same afternoon. Her next ambition is a winter league win, with enough private hours left to make a thoroughly unpromising painting.

Everyday responses

What matters most
She needs pursuits that belong to her, serious competition and family affection that survives a refusal. Being consulted matters more than being praised as indispensable.
Communication
Her speech is brisk, specific and frequently funny. She can finish somebody else’s argument before they have made it, then declare the matter settled. Genuine delight makes her less economical.
Decisions
She starts with a workable arrangement and commits quickly. In a match, attachment to a bold opening can make her discount fresh evidence that she would demand in somebody else’s plan.
Building trust
Keep an agreed appointment, play honestly and disagree without fussing over her. She trusts people who can enjoy her company without immediately requesting a favor.
Conflict & repair
Her first concession may be wrapped in a joke. A real repair means naming what she repeated or dismissed, apologizing to the affected person and resisting the urge to retell the apology.
Under stress
She identifies an external nuisance first: noise, timing, an awkward table. Feeling overclaimed makes her close discussion abruptly rather than explain how resentment has accumulated.
Giving & receiving care
She remembers preferences and fulfills particular promises. She enjoys being useful in a defined moment; she can also express love by taking someone seriously enough to beat them.
A useful contradiction
She fiercely protects her own private time while occasionally treating another person’s private story as excellent public material.

A continuing home

Familiar covered landings and the ferry hall give her easy access to company, competition and family. Her painting room can expand her life without requiring a different address.

Where it can feel less comfortable

That same familiarity encourages assumptions about her availability. A neighbor’s question can feel like an assignment before she has checked whether it actually is one.

In a small moment

A doubles partner challenges her opening.

You did warn me. You needn’t look so pleased about being right. Again?

She resets the dominoes and lets the partner propose the next approach.

Family boxes still occupy her painting space.

Saturday was the date. Pick yours up, and then you can tell me whether this blue is awful.

She points to the labeled boxes, then turns the unfinished canvas toward them.
