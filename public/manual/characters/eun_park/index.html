Eun Park

Retired school secretary & bird-call listener

Eun Park, sixty-six, has retired from school administration and stopped treating every morning as an appointment. She listens for birds on sheltered terraces and trades recordings with her daughter Yuna.

Background

East Asian; Korean-family background within Glasssteppe’s established railway community.

A life in detail

323 words

Her address notebook contains crossed-out terraces, phone numbers and people who moved back after everyone learned the new address. Eun kept similar order as a school secretary. After retirement, an acquaintance assumed she would enjoy managing a committee’s correspondence. She tried it, disliked the steady stream of requests and resigned. An empty morning had become something she wanted to keep.

Bird calls give her a reason to stand still without being available. One spring morning she confidently identified a repeated phrase, then checked her guide and discovered she had been wrong all week. Annoyed, she returned the following day. She now waits longer before checking, listening for the gaps as well as the notes. Some recordings contain mostly wind, footsteps and her impatient sigh.

Yuna, her older child, grew up in their Korean-background household before moving to Pelagia. A small recording can bring Eun pleasure without requiring a long call. Unanswered messages are harder: she sometimes reads silence as a measure of distance, then discovers Yuna was simply occupied. She has begun leaving a question until their next conversation rather than following it with three increasingly unnecessary updates.

Her son Min lives in Glasssteppe and co-parents ten-year-old Jiwoo. Eun once introduced him as the one who stayed, meaning affection and landing on a comparison he resented. He told her that work, parenting and friends were reasons, not evidence of which child cared more. She has stopped using the phrase, although changing the habit of measuring closeness takes longer. Jiwoo’s football commentary offers a welcome subject beyond family geography.

Eun has friends and chosen outings of her own, including terrace walks that end wherever the group finds shelter. Cold platforms irritate her; familiar stair lanes in summer still make her glad to live here. She follows through when she has agreed to meet someone. Tomorrow morning, however, remains unbooked, and she intends to decide whether to take the recorder only after opening the curtains.

Everyday responses

What matters most
Continuing connection, unhurried attention and a retired life with its own shape. She wants family closeness that survives distance without having to prove itself every day.
Communication
Specific observations and a remembered detail before the question. A worried question may arrive disguised as a practical check; her calmer voice can leave a call unfinished.
Decisions
Rely on what has worked, check the immediate conditions and preserve an unscheduled option. She is able to leave a committee even if somebody expected her to remain useful.
Building trust
Make realistic arrangements and keep them. She also needs to experience a delayed reply followed by ordinary warmth, rather than treating constant access as the only safe pattern.
Conflict & repair
She initially meant the comparison as praise. Her repair names each child’s choice separately and keeps the abandoned label out of later introductions, rather than merely explaining her good intention.
Under stress
Checks messages again or privately measures closeness by proximity. She has stopped calling Min the one who stayed; changing the underlying comparison takes longer than dropping the phrase.
Giving & receiving care
Sends a noticed sound or remembers a preference without requiring a response. Listening becomes more generous when it is not also a test of affection.
A useful contradiction
She resists a retired life on somebody else’s timetable while privately wanting family replies to arrive on hers.

A continuing home

Glasssteppe is her chosen continuing home, with active friendships and familiar terraces that support listening and walking at her own pace.

Where it can feel less comfortable

Cold platforms can make a simple outing disagreeable. Yuna’s move changes the ease of contact, while the phrase about Min staying risks making the city a family obligation.

In a small moment

Yuna has not replied to an ordinary message.

I’ll keep the recording. She can hear it when she has a quiet minute.

She returns to listening instead of sending another check.

Min objects to being called the one who stayed.

You chose a whole life here. I made it sound like you merely didn’t leave.

She acknowledges the distinction without comparing siblings again.
