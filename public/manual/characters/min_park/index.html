Min Park

Funicular electrician & amateur escape-room designer

Min Park is thirty-four, a funicular electrician and the father of ten-year-old Jiwoo. After her bedtime, he builds puzzle boxes that sometimes need fewer secrets than he gives them.

Background

East Asian; Korean-family background shared with Eun, Yuna and Jiwoo.

A life in detail

331 words

At work, Min values a clear maintenance record and enough time to finish safety checks properly. At home, puzzle components have expanded across two shelves and the table. He can locate a catch immediately but mislay the ordinary tape. Having the distinction pointed out irritates him, partly because it is true.

He grew up in Glasssteppe with his older sister Yuna and their mother Eun, in a Korean-background family rooted in the highland railway neighborhoods. Yuna’s move to Pelagia gave their conversations new places to imagine. It also made Min sensitive to being described as the steady sibling who stayed. He chose a workable combination of employment, friends and co-parenting. Curiosity did not leave the city with his sister.

Jiwoo divides her childhood routines between Min and her other parent, his former partner. The adults handle those arrangements, and his agreed parenting time is protected from optional projects. He likes hearing her improvised match commentary, though a tired evening can turn a harmless repetition into something he answers too sharply. He apologizes without asking her to make him feel better. He is less practiced at telling another adult he needs help before reaching that point.

His puzzle hobby began with a small box opened by arranging three clues. A later version added so many stages that friends spent more time listening to instructions than solving anything. Min initially blamed their impatience. Watching Jiwoo lose interest in another explanation made the problem harder to ignore. He has kept the elaborate mechanism for himself and started a shorter game for an evening he hopes people will pay to attend.

He plans one event, with a clear finish time and manageable preparation. He enjoys competition and wants his puzzles admired, so simplicity remains a negotiation. Glasssteppe’s compact streets let him reach work, shops and family without making every errand an expedition. He wants to use some of that saved time to play, rather than fill every available surface and hour with another clever obligation.

Everyday responses

What matters most
Recognition as a curious person in his own right, workable parenting and time for invention that stays pleasurable. Staying in Glasssteppe should not make him the family’s default reliable background.
Communication
Precise explanations, speculative branches and an occasionally triumphant reveal. He can forget that a listener wanted to play before hearing how clever the mechanism is.
Decisions
Test the logic and anticipate exceptions, then compare the result with what players actually enjoy. He needs a stopping point before another elegant layer becomes mandatory.
Building trust
Respect parenting time, ask a substantive question and credit the choice to stay without ranking it against travel. Direct offers with clear limits are easier to accept.
Conflict & repair
He first defends why the design makes sense. Repair means acknowledging that the experience became frustrating, removing an unnecessary layer and apologizing for the sharp remark itself.
Under stress
Complicates a game to prove himself, corrects too sharply or refuses help until he is visibly tired. A smaller question lets him regain proportion.
Giving & receiving care
Builds something amusing, solves a bounded practical problem or gives Jiwoo a real turn. Care also means allowing her interest to end before his explanation does.
A useful contradiction
He wants other people to delight in discovery and sometimes explains so much that he takes the discovery away.

A continuing home

Work, co-parenting and compact routes make staying in Glasssteppe an active adult decision. Its technical culture supplies materials for curiosity without prescribing his whole identity.

Where it can feel less comfortable

Dense family comparisons can make him feel less adventurous than Yuna. Turning a puzzle hobby into paid work risks reproducing the exhaustion he wanted it to relieve.

In a small moment

A player cannot follow his elaborate game.

I added that because I liked it. I haven’t proved it makes this more fun.

He removes one layer and tries the simpler version.

Jiwoo says the explanation is too long.

You wanted to try it. I gave you the whole instruction museum. Your turn.

He hands over the safe game and stops narrating.
