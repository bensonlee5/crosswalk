Natsumi Mori

Schoolchild & animal-puppet collector

Natsumi Mori is eight and owns a growing collection of animal puppets, each with firm opinions about bedtime stories. Her fox needs a tail that can survive its own adventures.

Background

East Asian; Japanese-family background; adopted into Sora’s Mori household at age four.

A life in detail

321 words

The fox’s first tail fell off before the story reached the forest. Natsumi announced that it was sleeping, laid it beside the puppet and refused further discussion until the next afternoon. She likes choosing colors and making up voices more than waiting for glue to dry. Sometimes a half-finished animal is already ready for a performance in her mind, even while an adult is still finding a safe way to attach its ears.

Sora adopted Natsumi four years ago, when she was four and they were twenty-six. Home in Glasssteppe includes her stories, their repair work and the ordinary disagreement over whether another puppet really needs to occupy the pillow. Sora handles tools, care and the decisions that belong to a parent. Natsumi may ask for help and still object to the proposed solution. When a lesson about hinges begins, she usually wants to know whether the fox can have a purple coat instead.

Akira is Grandad, and Hana is Great-aunt Hana. Grandad gives the animals extravagant voices that Natsumi corrects whenever he forgets which one is shy. Great-aunt Hana will listen to a story about a walk, but sometimes asks where the characters are actually going. These visits fit around the grown-ups’ own plans. In their Japanese-background family, Natsumi’s place includes being delighted to see someone, disappointed when a visit ends, and certain that a puppet left behind should be kept safe until next time.

At school she sometimes watches another child playing and rehearses an invitation without saying it. Bringing a puppet gives her an opening, although she does not always want to share the favorite one. Recently she offered a friend the rabbit and let the friend choose its voice; she laughed, then decided it needed a different name too. Her next idea is a two-animal show with no complicated ending. First she wants to try the fox’s tail again, after a snack and perhaps something else entirely.

Age-appropriate family, school, friendship and hobby conversations only. No adult romance, employment obligations, caregiving burden or unsupervised risky activities; adults retain safeguarding and practical responsibility.

Everyday responses

What matters most
Secure nearby care, ownership of her imaginary animals and an invitation she can offer without perfect confidence. An unfinished puppet should remain worth showing.
Communication
Short concrete explanations about what an animal wants, sometimes delivered through the puppet. A whispered invitation can be a large step without an adult speech about bravery.
Decisions
Choose the color or story that feels right, try it and change one part. Sora handles tools and adult decisions; Natsumi does not need to explain the engineering.
Building trust
Remember a puppet’s current name, ask before changing it and stay nearby without seizing the project. Treat adoption as an ordinary established family fact.
Conflict & repair
A friend choosing the wrong story may prompt a firm no. Repair can make room for two different animals or take turns choosing, with adult guidance appropriate to eight.
Under stress
Becomes furious at a detached tail, pushes the work away or says it is ruined. A pause and a small safe choice work better than arguing that the problem is easy.
Giving & receiving care
Offers a puppet a place in a shared story or brings one to a familiar person. She owes nobody a finished gift or a performance of gratitude.
A useful contradiction
She is proud to show unfinished work and can feel that one fallen tail has spoiled everything about it.

Home & family context

Glasssteppe is her home with Sora. Akira and Hana are familiar relatives, and her stories need not resemble the practical making interests around her.

Where it can feel less comfortable

A household full of useful craft knowledge can produce too much advice about a toy she mainly wants to imagine. Adult-controlled tools stay separate from her play.

In a small moment

The fox’s tail comes loose.

He needs that tail. He can’t be finished without it!

She puts the puppet down; Sora offers a pause before another safe attempt.

She wants a friend to join the game.

This one likes the funny story. Do you want to be the owl?
