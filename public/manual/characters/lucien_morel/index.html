Alder Quay

Lucien Morel

Retired ferry steward & chess-club contrarian

Lucien likes a well-played chess move, a ferry story with a verifiable ending and the pleasure of a spirited objection. Retirement has given him more time for all three.

Background

Black Afro-Atlantic Bellweather family; Solange’s father and Mina’s maternal grandfather.

A life in detail

323 words

Lucien spent most of his working life on the ferries and is now the person at chess club arguing that the old clock should stay. He enjoys the argument more than the clock. He lives independently near Solange and accepts help with a heavy shopping bag while objecting to unannounced rearrangements of his kitchen.

He is Solange’s biological father and Mina’s maternal grandfather, from a Black Afro-Atlantic Bellweather family. Solange’s mother brought Cuban-background Solenne connections into the household. Lucien remembers crossings between relatives as ordinary practical journeys, with missed boats and heavy bags as well as reunions. He dislikes a family account becoming so polished that nobody in it is ever inconvenient.

His objection to replacing the chess-club clock lasted three meetings. When someone finally demonstrated that the new one was easier to read, he conceded the point and proposed keeping the old clock on a shelf. He would prefer to say he was protecting club history. A friend suggests he simply enjoyed having something to argue about. Lucien considers both explanations respectable.

At home he is slowly matching photographs to ferry stories. A familiar face can bring back a whole shift; a date can still be wrong. He has started writing “uncertain” beside memories he cannot check, though the word annoys him. He hopes to record several accounts with Mina or Solange and leave enough room for them to challenge a detail.

He lives independently near Solange and accepts help carrying a heavy bag when he asks for it. Unannounced improvements to his kitchen are another matter. Alder’s familiar routes make a short outing satisfying, and the water remains a chosen pleasure rather than a background he has stopped seeing. He keeps chess appointments more reliably than family calls and knows that this distinction can hurt. His tenderness often arrives as a remembered favorite food or a carefully saved seat, followed by a joke before anybody can make too much of it.

Everyday responses

What matters most
He needs agency in his own home, worthwhile opponents and a version of memory that permits uncertainty. He wants experience to be heard without pretending that it makes every preference correct.
Communication
He offers a counterexample, a dry question or a story with an inconvenient detail. Tenderness often arrives sideways, followed by a joke that prevents anyone dwelling too long on the gesture.
Decisions
He tests proposals aloud, sometimes for pleasure rather than necessity. A direct demonstration is more persuasive than repeated assurances that a change is modern or good for him.
Building trust
Ask before moving his things, remember an agreement and challenge his facts fairly. Flattery about his accumulated wisdom is less convincing than taking a particular claim seriously enough to check.
Conflict & repair
He may concede by proposing a compromise that lets him keep the last word. A fuller repair includes noticing when an entertaining argument cost someone time or made them feel disregarded.
Under stress
He becomes more exacting about small arrangements and may argue the principle long after the practical issue is clear. Prolonging discussion lets him retain control of an otherwise unwanted change.
Giving & receiving care
He saves a seat, remembers a preference or offers a well-chosen detail. Keeping a family call as reliably as a chess appointment would sometimes communicate more affection than another polished anecdote.
A useful contradiction
He wants ferry stories checked against facts while sometimes defending a preference because the argument itself is too enjoyable to surrender.

A continuing home

Water, familiar routes and proximity to Solange support his longstanding independent home. He still actively enjoys these surroundings rather than treating them only as inherited habit.

Where it can feel less comfortable

Other people’s helpful improvements can threaten his preferred arrangements. He also risks using local familiarity as evidence that his way remains best after circumstances have changed.

In a small moment

Someone demonstrates a useful replacement chess clock.

All right, that is easier to read. The old one can still sit on the shelf and judge us.

He tries the new clock instead of reopening the same objection.

A photograph’s date cannot be verified.

Put “uncertain.” I dislike the word, but it’s doing honest work.

He leaves the date unresolved rather than choosing the most flattering story.
