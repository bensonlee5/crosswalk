Alder Quay

Imani Baptiste

Screen-print apprentice & delivery cyclist

Imani wants her posters noticed for their design. Between a screen-print apprenticeship and delivery shifts, she is deciding how far she needs to travel to feel independent.

Background

Black Afro-Atlantic Bellweather ancestry through both biological parents; part of the Baptiste family.

A life in detail

322 words

Imani prints band posters in a shared studio and pays her share with delivery shifts. She has a tender relationship with younger brother Noel and a touchy one with praise: she wants credit for composition, not for being Celia’s talented grandchild. She is considering a seasonal studio job in Solenne.

Dahlia is her mother, Celia her grandmother and Eli her uncle. She and Noel are full siblings within a Black Afro-Atlantic Bellweather family. Malik became her stepfather when she was sixteen. She appreciates that he asks before offering advice, though she sometimes hears a judgment in a perfectly ordinary question. Their easiest conversations happen while they are doing separate jobs in the same room.

A small band recently chose her roughest poster sketch over the polished version she preferred. Imani spent an irritated evening insisting they had missed the point, then noticed how clearly the rough layout could be read from across the studio. She kept the payment and changed the design. Learning to work for a client has required a different kind of confidence from defending a sketchbook page.

The printing itself can absorb her for hours. Sending an invoice or checking that a delivery fee covered the ride feels much less compelling, until rent makes it urgent. She is saving for a seasonal studio job in Solenne and has begun comparing real room costs with the appealing photographs. Leaving might broaden her work; it would also put distance between her and a family that knows every early version of her ambitions.

She keeps one weekly afternoon for Noel, who often wants backgrounds for a stop-motion scene. Sometimes they work; sometimes they buy a snack and disagree about a film. Imani is affectionate and protective without agreeing to become another parent. Alder’s wet cycling routes are exhausting, its studio friendships sustaining. For now, she is trying to finish a portfolio strong enough that staying or leaving can both be genuine choices.

Openness

Receptiveness to imagination, novelty and different experiences; not intelligence.

She tests visual combinations and is interested in a different studio environment. A rough composition can change her mind, although she may defend the polished version first.

Conscientiousness

2/5 · Lower

Preference for planning, organization and follow-through; not moral worth or how much work someone can do.

Absorbed design work gets sustained attention while invoices slip. Her weekly afternoon with Noel shows that inconsistency is selective, rather than a total inability to keep commitments.

Extraversion

Social initiation, expressiveness and appetite for stimulation; not kindness or social skill.

She prefers shared activity and studio friendships to explaining herself before an audience. Recognition matters, but she wants the work to carry much of the introduction.

Tender with Noel and capable of accepting client needs, she can nevertheless hear judgment in an ordinary question and become abruptly protective of her choices.

Comparison and ambiguous praise stay with her. She can brood over being misunderstood, then return to the work and discover that some criticism was useful.

Everyday responses

What matters most
She needs authorship, fair payment and room to change without constant comparison with her younger self. Family closeness should include curiosity about who she is becoming.
Communication
She is most precise when discussing an image: spacing, weight, what reads across a room. Personal explanations arrive more indirectly, sometimes as a joke with a sharper edge than she intended.
Decisions
She begins with what feels visually right, then tests it against actual use. For Solenne, attractive photographs now have to survive room costs and commuting realities; no decision is settled yet.
Building trust
Notice a particular design decision instead of announcing that she is talented. Ask before advising, and let an unfinished idea exist without immediately converting it into a career plan.
Conflict & repair
She may need an evening before acknowledging a useful point. Repair is concrete: send the corrected work, admit a misreading or reopen the conversation without requiring the other person to flatter her first.
Under stress
She withdraws into revision or argues the artistic principle behind a practical disagreement. An overdue invoice can become harder to open the longer she has avoided it.
Giving & receiving care
She gives attentive, bounded time and inventive help. With Noel she can make a background or simply buy a snack; care does not require producing something impressive every afternoon.
A useful contradiction
She wants people to judge the work on its merits, but a client’s independent preference can feel like a personal failure to understand her.

A continuing home

Studio friendships and family make Alder a real home while she considers a seasonal opportunity elsewhere. Her continued presence is not evidence that she has rejected travel or ambition.

Where it can feel less comfortable

Wet delivery rides, studio costs and constant family comparisons can make distance seem easier than a difficult conversation. She needs to distinguish those pressures when weighing departure.

In a small moment

Someone praises a poster through her family connection.

Thanks. Did you notice the lettering, though? That’s the bit I spent all night moving.

She points to the composition and risks asking for a specific response.

Noel wants more help than their afternoon allows.

I can do the harbor, or we can get chips. I haven’t got time for the entire ocean.

She lets him choose, with genuine interest rather than a lecture about responsibility.
