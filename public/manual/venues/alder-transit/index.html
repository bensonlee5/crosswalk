Worldbuilding notes

The history, architecture and community description below expand this fictional place. Gameplay facts are listed separately.

A local history

Tess’s evening dispatch work links this place with the café counter. Bram knows that a route can look simple on paper and still fail when a gate is locked or a resting place is missing. Together these established histories make the station a place of usable information, without claiming either person runs every transport service.

Architecture & everyday space

A low brick hall and a broad covered apron face the public routes. Noticeboards sit where arrivals can stop without standing in a working doorway. A repair stand and parcel surface give practical tasks their own edge of the hall rather than treating travelers as workshop traffic.

Weather & the setting

Wind off the water and sudden rain make roof depth and sheltered waiting important. The hall offers a dry place to read a timetable before stepping out toward an exposed landing.

Its place in community life

Tess’s dispatch work and Bram’s attention to usable directions give the station a grounded human character. A well-marked rest stop can matter more than a pretty shortcut. Some meetings become conversations across a distance, with room at a map desk for an unhurried call.
