A fictional maker of Crosswalk

Rainline

Quietly ruled pages, practical margins and paper that leaves room for a correction.

What they make

Priya wants legible notes protected in a separate sleeve. Bram needs room to correct a route. Kit borrows the proportions of an order slip, then makes them do a different job.

Luz chooses dark-blue paper for a sky sketch and leaves a margin for a less serious name. The same stationery maker serves all these habits without making the results a uniform house style.

A recognizable character

A thin cobalt line on wrappers, unobtrusive product numbers and generous blank areas. Dark-blue craft sheets form a separate range.

The maker’s story

Rainline supplied everyday rosters, docket pads and ledgers before extending the range to pocket notebooks and craft papers. Its products travel through offices, kitchens, schoolbags and gardens without deciding what belongs on the page.

A printed rule can become a useful starting point or a line an artist ignores. The brand belongs to the blank stock; Bram’s map, Kit’s comic and Luz’s chart belong to their makers.

Place in everyday life

Budget to mid-range refillable or replaceable stock; ordinary stationery that does not make its user’s private work a luxury object.

Materials & workmanship

Readable layouts and serviceable paper. Standard notebooks need separate weather protection; neither a blue wrapper nor the name makes every sheet waterproof.

Character perspectives

These are newly authored character perspectives, not quotations from completed scenes.

  • Priya

    Likes a plain page and protects it with a Whaleskin sleeve rather than trusting a suggestive product name.

  • Bram

    Treats a blank margin as room for a changed gate time or a question that still needs checking.

  • Kit

    Uses small order slips as a constraint that sharpens a comic, not as a rule about its subject.

  • Luz Vega

    Likes the dark paper while still wanting room for a deliberately ridiculous constellation.
