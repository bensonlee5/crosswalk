A fictional maker of Crosswalk

Dalloway

Modest, repairable hardware whose parts are often more memorable than the advertising.

What they make

Rowan’s basket is useful because it separates damp plants from dry books. A Dalloway name tells them where the basket came from, while its pale replacement strip records the work of keeping this particular one in use.

June’s proposed bell supplies the sharper joke: a successful repair can make an object better at doing something its owner would rather it stopped demanding.

A recognizable character

Stamped lettering, exposed sensible fasteners, brushed or chromed metal, and dark-green or natural-fiber carriers.

The maker’s story

Dalloway’s core business is bicycle hardware, with baskets growing from the same habit of making things carryable and accessible. A stamped name on a bell can remain legible long after its box is gone.

Owners tend to recognize the practical shape before the maker’s mark. The objects can be maintained and altered without becoming anonymous; a replaced strip or strap becomes part of the particular example’s history.

Place in everyday life

Middle-of-the-shelf working goods. Used examples are common enough that owning one does not imply a costly purchase.

Materials & workmanship

Sturdy where force is expected, mechanically straightforward and able to show wear. Springs, straps and woven parts still need attention; repairability does not mean indestructibility.
