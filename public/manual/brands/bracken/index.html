A fictional maker of Crosswalk

Bracken

Dark red, warm grey and large usable controls, from a bedroom keyboard to a substantial drum.

What they make

Amir cares about what a keyboard lets him try quietly. Rosa wants a radio dial she can reach while delivering an opinion about the commentary. The same maker serves active music-making and happily opinionated listening.

The Redbank story drum is a larger, costlier member of the same family. Its clear head makes Nell’s blue-check duvet more visually memorable than the discreet maker’s mark.

A recognizable character

Small cream maker’s marks, warm-grey controls and a deep oxblood option on larger instruments. Product names are secondary to plainly marked knobs and keys.

The maker’s story

Bracken grew around playable instruments and later added everyday listening equipment. The Rainroom keyboard and Touchline radio occupy different corners of that ordinary musical life.

The brand does not own the work made with its products. Amir’s arrangement and Nell’s playing remain theirs, including the choice to share none of it on a particular afternoon.

Place in everyday life

Mainly mid-range, with simpler older models circulating second-hand or on loan. A large drum is a meaningful purchase; borrowing a keyboard makes no claim about its borrower’s means.

Materials & workmanship

Dependable ordinary equipment with replaceable wear parts. Clear controls matter more than decorative complexity, although older plastics can scuff and rest fittings can loosen.

Character perspectives

These are newly authored character perspectives, not quotations from completed scenes.
