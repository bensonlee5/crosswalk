A fictional maker of Crosswalk

Kestrel

The cream bird on a blue packet turns up at tea tables and on tins with a second life.

What they make

Tess wants tea she can brew reliably for customers; Miro prefers his less strong. Farah reaches for the spiced pieces of Ferry Mix, while Inés favors the plain ones. An ordinary shared packet can support different tastes without somebody needing to win.

Kestrel’s widest reach is through repeat purchases and tables where several people interact with the same food. The reused containers keep that recognition alive after the original contents are gone.

A recognizable character

A cream bird on blue, with yellow corners for Lemon Rounds and rust corners for savory Ferry Mix. Tea strength is plainly numbered.

The maker’s story

Kestrel supplies familiar tea and biscuits through grocers in several regions. No. 2 Breakfast Tea, sweet Lemon Rounds and savory Ferry Mix share a recognizable blue package while making their contents easy to distinguish.

Tea tins and broad biscuit boxes often have an unceremonious second life. Oren’s buttons and Lin’s paper houses are personal uses of common packaging, not branded projects.

Place in everyday life

Ordinary pantry purchases. An old empty tin has little resale importance; personal use supplies most of its value.

Materials & workmanship

Straightforward everyday tea and biscuits with easily distinguished packets. The useful tins can chip or dent; reusing a box is an owner’s choice, not a premium feature.

Character perspectives

These are newly authored character perspectives, not quotations from completed scenes.

  • Tess

    Brews No. 2 strongly enough for customers who add milk.

  • Miro

    Prefers a lighter cup and will say if it has steeped too long.

  • Farah Hussain

    Likes the spiced twists in Ferry Mix during a radio drama.

  • Inés Valdés

    Chooses the plain pieces and has no obligation to share Farah’s taste.

  • Oren

    Values the empty tin’s useful shape and his own family’s button-saving habit.

  • Lin

    Finds the broad empty biscuit box useful for a paper town.
