# Crosswalk: lives, chances and legacies

New rooms opt into `lifeVersion: 1`; previous rooms retain their original rules and scoreboard. An eight-week game still has a definite ending. The new ending describes each person's aspiration, earned chapters, relationships, present footing and a concrete next opening. It does not award a points winner.

## Uncertainty and agency

The server samples seven unique arrivals from a ten-event pool for weeks 2–8. A constrained shuffled deck includes two or three unexpected opportunities and never three setbacks consecutively. These pacing choices are design hypotheses, not an experimentally established optimum. The future schedule and seed are omitted from API responses.

A shared event affects people differently according to their career, craft, resources, housing and history with Mina, Eli and Jo. Immediate response costs and effective consequences are shown. Responses have deterministic effects; there are no decorative success odds or hidden payout rolls. A free cautious response always remains available and is used if the player ends the week without responding. The end-week preview includes its effects.

Unexpected patrons, invitations and a temporary shop vacancy can suggest a different calling. Setbacks can change a role, strain a friendship, interrupt work or damage equipment. Trust, support, rivalry and outstanding favors persist and affect later choices. Honest check-ins and paid or free DIY repairs give players concrete recovery options.

## Achievement and hope

Players choose a direction in career, community, making or belonging and may revise it once per week. Named milestones require actual accomplishments such as mentoring, delivering a commission, helping complete the shared garden, or caring for a home. They remain part of a person's history after a setback, while the UI separately describes current financial and relational footing. Hopeful endings acknowledge debt, distance and unfinished work honestly instead of declaring every life settled.

## Evidence and limits

- Kang et al. (2009), *The Wick in the Candle of Learning*: curiosity in trivia tasks predicted willingness to spend resources to learn answers and later memory. Using specific unresolved human questions in a narrative is a design inference. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02402.x
- Shen, Fishbach & Hsee (2015), *The Motivating-Uncertainty Effect*: uncertain reward magnitude increased effort under process-focused pursuit, but not outcome-focused attention. This does not establish an optimal event frequency or directly test narrative arrivals. https://luxi-shen-4unh.squarespace.com/s/2015_ShenFishbachHsee_Uncertainty_GoalPersuit_JCR.pdf
- Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski (2006), *The Motivational Pull of Video Games*: autonomy and competence were associated with enjoyment and preference; relatedness findings came from multiplayer social connection. NPC relationships are an extrapolation. https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf
- Clark et al. (2009): gambling near-misses could increase desire to continue despite being less pleasant. Crosswalk does not use deceptive near-misses or apparent control over chance. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19217383/

Evaluate enjoyment, fairness, curiosity, meaningful influence and whether a player can name a hopeful next step. Session duration or repeat play alone is not evidence of a better experience. Automated engine/DOM/API testing cannot establish those human outcomes.

## Places that keep living

New rooms also opt into `worldVersion: 1`. Each player begins with five essential local places. Actions, conversations and passing weeks reveal optional venues. Regional journeys cost a time block and a visible fare; ordinary local visits remain free. Choosing to leave trades familiar access for a smaller new map. Returning preserves personal history while the place itself develops.

Alder Quay, Saffron Reach, Glasssteppe, Pelagia and Ember Mesa have distinct architecture, destination paintings, language familiarity, food, residents and seasonal weather. Fourteen regional residents have repeat conversations and persistent bonds. Small towns give familiar people several jobs. Neri’s invitations and partnership are optional choices; no relationship automatically relocates a player.

Weather uses the public room code, region and chapter so server costs and client previews agree without exposing the private life-event deck. Essential work, rest and community venues remain available even when money or energy is exhausted. Regional discoveries, venue changes and voluntary closures survive reloads and are specific to the player’s journey.

Every return records an exact, previewed local change: a newly accessible venue or an added use at an existing one, a resident’s changed circumstances, and a one-time paid opening. Departures retain the local activity and bond history used to shape the welcome. Return jobs use one time block and pay $55–70; repeated travel cannot create a free reward loop. Essential venues and deliberately closed venues remain protected.
