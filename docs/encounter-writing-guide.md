# Encounter writing and voice

## Reading experience

The editorial target is literature accessible to a reader around grades 10–12: concrete, precise language; consequences the reader can infer; and syntax capable of holding more than one competing interest. This is not a requirement to make every character sound academic. Children retain their ages. A tired adult, a direct refusal, or a practical instruction may need very few words.

Show the essential premise, the participants’ competing wants, and the immediate practical stakes before the first choice. An object can carry several meanings without the narrator explaining anybody’s personality: the same drum is an instrument to Nell, occupied floor space to Jonah, and an opportunity for a joke. Appearance belongs to the image. Prose supplies pressure, hesitation, action, history relevant to the decision, and what changes after it.

A scene should not require paying for successive introductory sentences. Reading the opening, examining a choice, and revealing an authored clarification are presentation-only. A confirmed decision retains its exact action title, outcome, and speaker response until the reader continues. It may change the next scene, but the next scene must not displace the answer just received.

The 46 main ensemble scenes have authored opening exchanges. All 138 main alternatives have exact, branch-specific spoken replies; their public effect strings, economics, predicates, source ownership, and action IDs are unchanged. The existing 60-stage personal aftermath remains intact. The 47 adult portrait profiles have fuller first and familiar greetings; six minors remain age-appropriate. The 26 core adults have 130 authored response variants, and the seven Quay phases have scene-specific speech for all three participants.

## Validation and remaining pacing questions

`scripts/audit-encounter-writing.mjs` reports approximate Flesch–Kincaid values for sufficiently large aggregates. Its vowel-group syllable counter and punctuation-based sentence count are deliberately labeled approximate; names, contractions, quoted turns and performance cadence make it unsuitable as a certification or a quality gate. The reading-level goal is editorial. Review clarity and variation before accepting a score.

All economic costs remain unchanged in this pass. The paid Nell bed-clarification branch settles a different sleeping arrangement rather than merely defining the premise; the optional duvet question is free. Mara’s report branch remains one priority block, while explaining why the outlet matters is free. Future cost consolidation should examine these exact actions and their real effects, preserve work and payment terms, and avoid repeatable free gains.

Exact recorded audio must bind to the displayed speech text and the canonical speaker ID. Narration and stage directions are separate fields; changing the speech invalidates a recording rather than silently playing older words. A receipt, not a preview or later poll, establishes the spoken outcome.
